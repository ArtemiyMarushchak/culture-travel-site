27 September · Day 1

Johannesburg, Lion Park and dinner at Carnivore

Your first encounter with South Africa begins with a gentle immersion into African nature, the atmosphere of safari and the local culinary culture.

Arrival in Johannesburg. Recommended flight: EK 761 arriving at 10:15.

After passport control and baggage claim — meet our driver at the airport and begin your introduction to South Africa.

Rather than lose the day waiting for hotel check-in after the flight, we head straight to Lion Park — one of the best-known nature parks near Johannesburg.

Across more than 600 hectares live lions, including rare white lions, along with other members of African fauna.

This makes for a soft, atmospheric first immersion into African nature before the journey continues across the country.

After the visit — transfer and hotel check-in.

In the evening — dinner at the celebrated restaurant Carnivore, one of South Africa’s best-known culinary landmarks. The atmosphere of African safari, dishes cooked over an open fire, and a first taste of local gastronomic culture set the perfect mood for the start of the journey.

Dinner is included in the programme price
drinks and desserts are paid separately.

28 September · Day 2

Journey along the Panorama Route

After breakfast — departure toward one of South Africa’s most scenic regions — Mpumalanga.

Today you travel the famous Panorama Route — a scenic drive widely regarded as one of the most beautiful road journeys in the country.

The road winds through mountain passes, green hills, viewpoints and canyons, gradually revealing a wholly different South Africa — natural, vast and remarkably diverse.

Along the way, stops are planned at the region’s best-known natural landmarks:

Blyde River Canyon — the third-largest canyon in the world and one of South Africa’s most striking natural landscapes.

God’s Window — a viewpoint from which, on a clear day, endless panoramas of valleys and mountains unfold.

Mpumalanga waterfalls — scenic cascades framed by dense vegetation and rock formations.

En route — a light lunch included in the price.
Drinks are paid separately.

By evening — check-in at a hotel set among the mountain landscapes of Mpumalanga. The hotel is known for its panoramic views of valleys and sunsets, creating a distinctive atmosphere of calm and full immersion in South African nature.

29 September · Day 3

Transfer to a private safari reserve

After breakfast — transfer to the private safari reserve Mjejane.

The private safari reserve Mjejane lies within Greater Kruger — one of Africa’s largest and most renowned national parks.

Kruger National Park is famous for its vast wilderness, rich wildlife and the chance to observe African animals in their natural habitat.

Travellers from around the world come here for a true safari.

On arrival — check-in at a lodge on the riverbank, where animals often come to drink.

In the evening, your first safari awaits on open jeeps with professional rangers.

At sunset the savannah comes especially alive: this is when you may encounter elephants, buffalo, giraffes, antelope, and predators setting out to hunt.

As evening falls, the safari takes on a distinctive atmosphere — soft sunset light, the sounds of African nature and the first truly powerful emotions of meeting wild Africa.

After the safari — dinner and rest at the lodge.

30 September · Day 4

In search of the African Big Five

Morning and evening safari in one of South Africa’s best-known reserves.

Kruger Park is the oldest and most famous reserve in South Africa.

It lies in the north-east of the country, across the provinces of Mpumalanga and Limpopo, and covers nearly 12,000 square kilometres.

The landscapes are highly varied: veld grasslands, river valleys, foothills and savannah.

The reserve is home to nearly five hundred bird species and more than a hundred reptile species.

Here you have the greatest chance of encountering the full African Big Five: lion, rhinoceros, elephant, buffalo and leopard.

In addition, many elephants, giraffes, zebras, cheetahs, antelope and other African animals live here.

Approximate day schedule: early rise, snacks + tea/coffee in the lodge lobby, morning safari, return to the lodge for breakfast, free time to rest, lunch, evening safari.

2 October · Day 6

Hermanus and whale watching

After breakfast — an excursion to Hermanus with a Russian-speaking guide.

Hermanus is a small coastal town regarded as one of the best places in the world for whale watching.

The road follows the scenic ocean coastline of the Western Cape with beautiful panoramas of the Atlantic, mountains and coastal bays.

On arrival — a marine cruise for whale watching as part of a small international group with a local English-speaking escort.

You set out by boat in search of whales and come so close to these enormous mammals that you can hear their breathing and see the curiously shaped growths on their bodies.

Besides the southern right whale, there is a chance of seeing a humpback and an orca, as well as dolphins and seals.

Return to Cape Town.

3 October · Day 7

Cape Town and Table Mountain

A sightseeing tour of Cape Town and an ascent of Table Mountain with a Russian-speaking guide.

Fast-track for the cable car is included.

Your day begins with an ascent of Table Mountain — one of the Seven Natural Wonders in the heart of Cape Town.

With breathtaking views of the city, the Twelve Apostles mountain range, Lion’s Head and the ocean, as well as diverse flora and historical significance, the flat-topped mountain has won the hearts of millions of visitors from around the world.

Next comes an introduction to the city of wanderers and adventurers.

Cape Town is a vivid blend of cultures, striking architecture and rich history set against the beauty of the surrounding landscape.

You will visit the heart of the city’s most compelling landmarks, including the colourful Malay quarter of Bo-Kaap, the Castle of Good Hope, District Six and the Company’s Garden.

Entrance tickets to the Castle of Good Hope and the Bo-Kaap Museum are included.

5 October · Day 9

Cape of Good Hope and the penguins of Boulders

A cruise to Seal Island, an excursion to the Cape of Good Hope and a visit to Boulders penguin beach with a Russian-speaking guide.

A scenic drive along the ocean shore and the white beaches of Camps Bay leads to the colourful fishing village of Hout Bay.

From Hout Bay harbour you set out on a 40-minute cruise to Duiker Island, also known as Seal Island, where you can see thousands of Cape fur seals in their natural habitat. As part of a small international group with a local English-speaking escort.

Next you travel one of the world’s most spectacular coastal switchbacks — Chapman’s Peak Drive.

Winding along the Atlantic Ocean from Hout Bay to the beaches of Noordhoek, the 9 km road has 114 bends and follows the rocky coastline around Chapman’s Peak.

You will also visit the Cape of Good Hope, discovered in 1488 by Bartolomeu Dias, with a rich history from the age of great navigators and explorers.

The excursion concludes at Boulders Beach, nestled among a chain of beautiful secluded coves and beaches near the scenic village of Simon’s Town.

Boulders Beach is a place worth seeking out. No visit to Cape Town is complete without meeting this famous colony of African penguins.

6 October · Day 10

The wine valleys of Franschhoek and Stellenbosch

After breakfast — departure from Cape Town to the famous wine valleys of the Western Cape.

This is one of South Africa’s most beautiful and gastronomically renowned regions.

The road passes through scenic vineyards, mountain passes and historic towns with a distinctly European character.

The first stop is Franschhoek — a charming wine town founded by French Huguenots in the 17th century and regarded as South Africa’s culinary capital.

Here you visit a wine estate for a tasting of local wines amid vineyards and mountain scenery.

Next — transfer to Stellenbosch and a visit to the legendary wine estate Delaire Graff Estate — one of the country’s most beautiful and prestigious wine properties.

The estate is known for its wine collection, contemporary art, panoramic views and high culinary standards.

A table will be reserved for us for lunch at the estate restaurant. Lunch is paid separately according to the menu on site.

In the afternoon — check-in at a hotel on the grounds of a wine estate among the vineyards and mountains of the Western Cape.

7 October · Day 11

Leisure among the vineyards

A free day to rest among the vineyards and slowly discover the atmosphere of the Western Cape wine valleys.

The Spier estate is ideal for quiet leisure: walks among the vineyards, culinary experiences, a spa visit or simply enjoying nature and beautiful views.

For wine lovers, Spier offers a memorable experience — the Wine Blending Experience.

During the masterclass you can create your own signature wine by blending several varieties.

Once crafted, the wine is poured into an individual branded bottle of the estate, sealed with wax and beautifully presented — a souvenir that becomes a special reminder of your African journey.

For guests who do not drink alcohol, the winery offers separate non-alcoholic tastings with gastronomic pairings.

During the tasting experience you can try non-alcoholic wines and pair them with chocolate, cheeses, honey and other local delicacies.

Time in the wine valleys makes a beautiful, calm close to the journey through South Africa — after safari, ocean landscapes and full excursion days, it is especially rewarding to slow down, enjoy nature, gastronomy and the atmosphere of the Western Cape.
